# Competitive Intelligence & Strategic Bet · Roamly Groups

## 1. Primary competitor

- **Competitor:** DIY Travel Planning Stack (WhatsApp + Venmo + Splitwise + Google Docs/Sheets)
- **Core offering:** A collection of free, familiar tools that friends use to coordinate trips, discuss options, track expenses, split payments, and share travel plans.
Examples:
WhatsApp / iMessage → communication
Google Docs / Sheets → planning and coordination
Splitwise / Venmo → expense tracking and payments
Google Calendar → scheduling
- **How they position themselves:** They do not position themselves as a travel-planning solution.
The perceived value by users is:
Use the tools you already have to coordinate everything for free.
- **Primary audience:** Friend groups planning vacations
Bachelor/Bachelorette trips
Family vacations
Student travel groups
Any traveler coordinating plans across multiple people
- **Known pricing or packaging:** Free/Freemium
- **Why they're the biggest threat:** Unlike Airbnb or Viator, the DIY travel-planning stack is already the default behavior for our target segment.
Our users aren't choosing between Roamly Groups and another travel platform. They're choosing between:
Continuing to coordinate trips across multiple familiar apps
Moving planning, payments, and booking into a single Roamly experience
The DIY approach wins because:
It's already installed
Everyone knows how to use it
There is no onboarding friction
It's perceived as free
The DIY approach loses because:
Information gets fragmented
Payment collection is manual
Availability coordination is painful
One organizer carries most of the workload
Booking completion rates are lower

## 3. What I want to revisit in my Module 1 strategy

I want to revisit my value proposition because the battlecard showed that our biggest competitor isn't another travel platform, it's the DIY combination of WhatsApp, Venmo, Splitwise, and Google Docs. My original value proposition describes the segment and its challenges, but it doesn't clearly communicate why travelers should switch from tools they already use. I would refine it to focus on Roamly's unique advantage: helping friends plan, coordinate, pay, and book experiences in one place instead of juggling multiple apps.

## 4. Strategic bet

### Data
- **Who Roamly Groups is for:** I'm doubling down on group travel organizers and community leaders who repeatedly coordinate trips for others:
Alumni groups
Special-interest communities (hiking, cycling, photography, wellness)
Travel clubs
Corporate retreat organizers
Friend groups with one "trip captain"
These buyers experience the highest coordination pain and have the strongest incentive to pay for a better solution.
- **The one thing worth choosing it for:** Roamly Groups turns group trip coordination into a single workflow instead of a collection of disconnected tools.
Today organizers stitch together:
WhatsApp for communication
Google Sheets for planning
Venmo for payments
Email for updates
Multiple travel booking sites
Roamly Groups can become the system of record for the entire group trip.
- **The biggest threat to that position:** The biggest threat is the convenience of existing habits.
Competitors don't win because they're better. They win because everyone is already using WhatsApp, Facebook Groups, Google Docs, and Venmo. Convincing a group organizer to switch requires a significantly better experience, not just feature parity.

### Insight
The strongest signal is that the leading competitors are winning on adoption and convenience, not on travel-specific functionality.
Most organizers tolerate fragmented tools because getting everyone onto one platform is hard.

Instead of competing on "more travel features," Roamly Groups should compete on:
Reducing organizer workload.
The winning message is not:
"Plan travel better."
The winning message is:
"Save 10+ hours of group coordination per trip."
Competitors focus on communication.
Roamly Groups should focus on coordination and execution.

### Belief
I now believe that Roamly Groups wins when it becomes the easiest way for a group organizer to coordinate travel, because organizers feel the pain of planning far more acutely than the travelers they serve.

### Bet
- **I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by:** I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by becoming the operating system for group travel organizers, combining planning, communication, itineraries, payments, and attendee management into a single experience.
- **To make this bet, I am deprioritizing:** I would deprioritize:
Solo travel planning
Travel inspiration feeds
Social discovery features
General-purpose messaging
Consumer social networking functionality
